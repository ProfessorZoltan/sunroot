# Proposal: Rainforest Gardens

A sixth biome, Rainforest Gardens, for the playtester's review. It comes out of the research on regenerative systems
([research/regenerative-systems.md](../research/regenerative-systems.md)), whose humid tropics
section ends: "in hot, wet climates nutrients are held most safely in charcoal and living plants,
not in bare soil, so the systems keep the ground covered and stacked." The biome is built from
the Chagga home gardens of Kilimanjaro, the Kandyan forest gardens of Sri Lanka, the Maya
milpa-forest garden cycle, Amazonian terra preta and the Balinese subak. Everything here is a
proposal: the designer's defaults until review, the bots and playtesting tune them.

**Status**: started. Decided by the playtester: the biome is called Rainforest Gardens; it joins once 10 districts stand in Root City; the other open questions take the proposal's defaults (layers inside one building, coffee and pepper as materials, fire as a risk only).

It builds on what the earlier biomes left: the land-health ladder (barren, scrub, meadow,
woodland) and compost; evolutions with an age (`when.minAge`, the Rice-Duck Paddy); per-tile
counters kept on the map (a ruin's salvage, the lake's mud); charred tiles (the Highland's
biochar, +1 food for a farm); the shade rule (tall things dim solar beside them); cooling on hot
days (the desert's wind towers); edge pieces (hedgerows, willow edges); and the lessons of the
others: check the wonder fits real maps, give the land a steady source of materials, propose a
Year 1 the numbers can pay for, and run the balance gate over 40 seeds, not 20.

## What makes it different

| Elsewhere                                                        | Forest Garden                                                                                                                                                  | Source                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| One building to a tile; combos come from neighbours side by side | **Layers**: a forest garden grows upward, up to four storeys on one tile, each helping the ones below. The puzzle is vertical as well as across the map.       | Research: Chagga, Kandyan      |
| Land heals up the ladder and stays healed                        | **Fertility washes out**: in the monsoon, bare farmed soil loses what it holds. Land kept under a canopy, or turned to dark earth, keeps it.                   | Research: humid tropics lesson |
| Woodland is a tile to keep for Harmony                           | Woodland is most of the map, and **clearing it is a trade**: a burned clearing is rich for a few years and then poor, unless it is layered back into a garden. | Research: Maya milpa           |
| Compost is spread and used up                                    | **Dark earth** is made for good: household waste and charcoal round the homes turn the ground black and fertile, and it never washes out.                      | Research: terra preta          |

The signature choice: **clear or layer**. Clearing forest is fast food and costs Harmony; the
ash fertility washes away in a few monsoons. A layered garden is slow to grow and keeps both the
Harmony and the soil. A settlement that clears too much burns through its land.

## The new rules

Three rules, each contained, each reusing machinery the game already has.

| Rule           | How it works                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Reuses                                                                                  | Source                                         |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Layers**     | A Forest Garden is one building with up to 4 layers: **ground** (taro, beans, vegetables), **shrub** (coffee or cacao), **understory** (banana) and **canopy** (jackfruit, coconut, mango). It is placed with its ground layer; the others are added to it later for a cost, one a season, like the coppice action. Each layer makes its own yield, takes its own seasons to grow (the canopy 4), and helps the layers below: a canopy shades the shrub (+1), the understory's leaves mulch the ground (+1), and with all four the garden's tile never washes out. A layer needs no extra worker: a garden is cheap to keep once it grows. | Evolutions with an age; the coppice action; a building's own state, as a wonder's stage | Research: Chagga (four storeys), Kandyan       |
| **Fertility**  | A farmed tile holds fertility, 0 to 4, kept on the tile as the lake's mud. Clearing woodland by burning gives the new field 3 (ash). A milpa makes 1 more food for each point. In the monsoon (summer) every farmed tile with no canopy over it loses 1; a field at 0 makes half and drops a step down the land-health ladder each monsoon after. Compost spread on it adds 1, as healing does elsewhere.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | A per-tile counter, as salvage and mud; the land-health ladder; compost                 | Research: Maya milpa, the humid tropics lesson |
| **Dark earth** | A Kitchen Midden beside homes takes their scraps and the ash and charcoal of a Char Hearth. Each year it turns one tile within 1 of it into **dark earth** (a new tile type, after the nearest tiles first), then within 2 (the lighter _terra mulata_). Dark earth is fertile land that never loses fertility, gives any farm on it +1 food (the charred tile's bonus), and stays dark if the midden goes.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Scraps (people already make them); charred tiles; `setsTile`                            | Research: terra preta                          |

## Map

About 120 tiles, from a seed. Rainforest over most of it (about 60%), a river winding through
with its seasonal floodplain, a few natural clearings, hills on one side, and an old plantation
(a ruined monoculture estate: barren ground and ruins, the biome's steady source of materials).

## Seasons and events

The tropics have a wet and a dry season, not four temperate ones. The game's four map onto them.

| Season | Event       | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Source                                 |
| ------ | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Spring | First rains | The planting season: milpas sown this season make 1 more food in summer. A quiet event, to teach.                                                                                                                             | Research: milpa                        |
| Summer | Monsoon     | Heavy rain: farmed tiles with no canopy over them lose 1 fertility; the river floods its floodplain (silt, as the Reach's flood). Homes need 1 cooling on summer days (humid heat), unless a canopy layer stands beside them. | Research: humid tropics; SD1 (cooling) |
| Autumn | Cyclone     | Wind: 2 exposed buildings may be damaged; a canopy layer with nothing taller round it may fall (it drops back to understory). A living fence along the windward edge shelters it.                                             | DESIGN.md (storms)                     |
| Winter | Dry season  | Fire risk: a cleared tile (milpa, scrub) beside rainforest may catch, burning one neighbour from forest to scrub, unless a living fence or a firebreak lies between. Solar is strong; the river runs low.                     | Research: cultural burning (as a risk) |

## Energy and cooling

No heat is needed: the tropics are warm all year. Cooling is the desert's rule, lighter: homes
need 1 on summer days, and a canopy beside a home cools it for free (shade), so the forest itself
is the cheapest cooling.

| Source          | Cost | Placement        | Day (spring to winter)       | Night         | Notes                                                                                                 | Source            |
| --------------- | ---- | ---------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Micro-hydro     | 5    | Beside the river | 3 / 4 / 3 / 1                | 3 / 4 / 3 / 1 | Strong in the monsoon, weak in the dry season: the mirror of solar.                                   | New               |
| Solar Canopy    | 4    | Open land        | as the Reach's, +1 in winter | —             | A canopy layer beside it shades it (the shade rule), so panels want clearings: canopy against panels. | DESIGN.md (shade) |
| Biogas Digester | 6    | Anywhere         | —                            | —             | Shared, fed well by the Stall Barn's manure.                                                          | EXPANSION.md      |

Shared as the Reach: Cell Bank, Wind Spire (weak here), Heat Well (not needed), Solar Thermal
Collector (not needed). Absorption chillers and wind towers stay in the desert.
